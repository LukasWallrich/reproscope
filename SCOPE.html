reproscope

An open-source instrument that puts a social-science paper through a pipeline of escalating scrutiny: does it compute, is it sound, does it hold up. It needs only the paper and its data, not the original analysis code.

Status: experiment. This project is exploratory and open from the start. It may or may not yield a broadly useful tool. Findings about where it fails count as deliverables alongside the tool itself.

Positioning

reproscope is an open review-stage instrument that extends the Kohler et al. (2026, arXiv:2604.21965) re-derivation design from verdict benchmarking to robustness measurement. It replaces single-shot reproduction with a k-replica agreement statistic and locates the paper-stated specification within its defensible analysis space.

Kohler et al. already established the core re-derivation machinery on 48 pre-certified reproducible I4R papers: vision-based extraction from rendered pages, results-redacted methods documents with a numeral leak-check, blinded implementation templates, tolerance grading, and an unblinded diagnosis pass. reproscope adopts those elements with attribution rather than re-inventing them. It makes three contributions of its own:

  • Multiverse + extremeness measure. Kohler et al. classify robustness to alternative specifications as a reproducibility type they did not address and name it as future work. Their observation that agents succeed when "implicit assumptions happen to align with the original implementation" describes exactly the quantity the multiverse measures. Adjacency, not proof of feasibility — that is what the pilot tests.
  • k-replica dispersion as a primary statistic. Their agreement analysis requires ground truth, which does not exist for an unvetted paper. Replica-to-replica dispersion needs no target, and their finding that about half of coefficients differ from themselves across repeated runs is the case for k>1 (it does not by itself justify any particular k or decomposition; the pilot calibrates that).
  • A review-stage instrument, not a benchmark study. Their corpus was pre-certified reproducible, so their grades never had to answer "what does a C mean for an unknown paper?". reproscope must, which forces explicit abstention states and a report that separates decision-relevant output from context.

Planned: contact Kohler and Ash. No repo or data-availability statement exists, their per-cause failure counts are unpublished, and this extension sits on the axis they disclaimed, so a collaboration or materials-sharing ask is natural. Independently, the ERROR project (Elson/Hussey) holds the ideal psychology corpus of expert-confirmed errors with no LLM arm. Evaluating reproscope's Stage 2 against it would itself be a contribution.

What it does

Intake & extraction → Stage 1 · Computation → Stage 2 · Correctness → Stage 3 · Robustness → Report

Framing rule, stated in every report header: Stage 1's match table is decision-relevant. Stages 2 and 3 are context for human evaluation and are labeled as such, never as verdicts.

The inter-stage contract

Every stage consumes and produces typed, versioned artifacts, and every downstream output retains the claim identifier it descends from. The chain:

ClaimRecord → EstimandContract → DataReadinessRecord → ReplicaDecisionTrace → ComparableResult → SpecificationSpace

Each artifact records provenance, confidence, failure state, and the exact upstream artifact it used. Every stage may return abstained instead of a result, and abstention propagates: a claim whose estimand cannot be bound to the data produces no match band, no dispersion statistic, and no multiverse — a recorded abstention with a reason, not a guess. Batch runs therefore finish in one of three states per claim: complete, abstained, or human_review_required. Unattended operation is a measured research hypothesis, not a promise: the report tracks abstention rate, escalation rate, and (on ground-truth corpora) the silent-error rate.

Stage 0 — Intake, extraction & data readiness

Two vision models, cross-checked · deterministic guards · abstention-first
  • Input is a per-paper manifest: PDF, data file(s), codebook (required in the pilot; optional later, with absence tracked as a measured failure mode), optional focal claim. Default claim scope is all inferential claims in the abstract; the pilot runs one user-selected focal estimand per paper. No auto-fetching in v1.
  • Each claim becomes a typed ClaimRecord → EstimandContract: claim → study/sample → outcome and exposure → estimand → table/figure cell → dataset variables → scale/transformation → uncertainty quantity. Multi-study papers get per-study contracts; a claim that cannot be pinned to a single estimand and cell is escalated or abstained, not approximated.
  • Two independent vision models extract the claims table; agreement is checked field by field (cell identity, value, estimand — not just wording). Cross-model agreement is treated as a confidence signal, not validation: disagreement or low confidence yields human_review_required. On ground-truth corpora, extraction accuracy is scored directly.
  • A data-readiness check inventories files, infers and cross-checks the schema (unit of observation, keys, missing-value sentinels, scale direction, weights, derived variables), records variable provenance, and abstains when the estimand contract cannot be bound to the data.
  • The stage produces a results-redacted methods document under a typed redaction policy: design numerals (years, thresholds, scale ranges, lags, sample rules) are permitted; outcome information is prohibited in any form — values, rounded variants, intervals, significance stars, signs, and verbal directions ("negative and significant"), including in captions and methods-interleaved passages. Enforcement is layered: an LLM redactor, the deterministic value scan (which blocks the run on any hit), and a semantic leakage audit on a validation sample, since the deterministic scan alone cannot carry the blinding guarantee. The redacted variant ships with the report so the blinding can be audited.
  • Deterministic checkers (metacheck modules, statcheck on source formats, GRIM/SPRITE via scrutiny/rsprite2) run here. Their output lands in the report appendix, with coverage stats printed next to the flags. (Phase 2 — see Phasing.)
Vision extraction, redaction and leak-check: adopted from Kohler et al. (three teams independently found vision beats the PDF text layer). New: the two-model cross-check, the estimand contract, the data-readiness check, and the typed redaction policy.

Stage 1 — Computation (decision-relevant)

k = 6 blinded replicas: 3 cheap model families × 2 runs
  • Each replica independently re-implements the analysis from the redacted methods, the estimand contract, and the data. Replicas never see the paper's results: supplying them is the documented fabrication trigger (accuracy on non-reproducible tasks drops from 100% to 63% when the PDF is provided) — necessary, though not by itself sufficient, for blinding (hence the layered redaction above). A hardcoded-results audit, taken from Kohler, checks each replica's code.
  • Each replica emits a ReplicaDecisionTrace: filters, transformations, model formula, missingness handling, weights, estimator settings. Dispersion is reported on two levels — decision-vector agreement and numeric-output agreement.
  • What the statistic is: replica dispersion conditional on the shared extraction and toolchain. It is not a direct measure of methods-section underspecification: high agreement can be shared training priors or a common mistake, low agreement can be agent randomness or extraction error, and common-mode Stage 0 errors are invisible to it. With 3×2 the within/between-family split stays descriptive (one within-family pair per family; dependent between-family pairs) — no variance decomposition is claimed. Whether dispersion predicts correctness is calibrated on ground-truth corpora before any interpretive weight is put on it.
  • Families: glm-5.3-flash, gpt-5.6-luna, third to be settled by the calibration run (not Haiku; deepseek-v4-flash qualifies since replicas read redacted markdown, so vision only binds Stage 0).
  • Stochasticity is layered, not lumped: (1) agent/run variation — the k replicas; (2) specification variation — Stage 3's multiverse; (3) Monte Carlo/execution variation — any stochastic estimation (bootstrap, MCMC, imputation) is rerun over controlled seeds until its Monte Carlo uncertainty is estimated, and replica comparisons use that distribution. Seeds and convergence noise never enter the multiverse; only substantively defensible algorithm choices do.
  • Execution happens in one pinned Docker image (R and Python scientific stacks). When a paper names specific versions, that replica gets a matched environment. Version-sensitive defensible defaults (estimator, optimizer choice) become named multiverse dimensions.
  • Matching uses quantity-specific equivalence rules, with the Kohler bands (sign gate, round to reported precision, A <2% / B <20% / C <40%, absolute threshold <0.002 when |x| < 0.001) as the base scheme for unbounded coefficients only; p-values, odds ratios, standardized effects, and bounded or transformed statistics get their own rules, and unit rescaling is checked before grading. The report always shows raw and standardized differences, the paper's reported precision, and uncertainty next to any band — a band summarizes, it does not adjudicate.
  • What a match means, stated in the report: a close match shows one route to the reported number exists; a mismatch does not identify whether the paper or the replicas are wrong — it triggers the diagnosis pass and is read jointly with the dispersion statistic and Stage 3. When valid original code exists, the reconstruct-and-re-run arm is reported as a categorically stronger form of evidence, quarantined from the blind arms (phase 2).
  • Divergence diagnosis: one unblinded top-model pass over the paper, all replica traces and code, and the re-execution arm where present, writing "plausible sources of divergence". Labeled conjecture, placed below the match table.
  • Reporting is broken down by analysis family (OLS/ANOVA, multilevel, SEM, meta-analysis, …). The pilot declares a small family (OLS/GLM); later phases add families with no a-priori exclusions — the tool's own reliability limits per family are findings.
Blinding, templates, tolerance bands, diagnosis pass: adopted from Kohler et al. New: the k-replica dispersion design, decision traces, and quantity-specific matching.

Stage 2 — Correctness (context)

Rubric-constrained LLM checks · no open-ended flaw-hunting
  • Three rubrics in v1, each with an external anchor: causal language vs design strength (the Haber CLAIMS rubric, validated with human-rated reference data); a minimum-detectable-effect / sensitivity curve under explicit, stated assumptions (variance structure, clustering, attrition) — not post-hoc power, which is uninformative; and claim–analysis alignment, judged against the paper-derived estimand contract, with each replica's differences from that contract stated separately. Generalization-scope and measurement-validity rubrics are deferred.
  • Precision expectation: the 70–85% figure from scope-constrained error-checking is a planning extrapolation from narrower tasks, not evidence about these rubrics. No measured number exists for causal-overclaiming or design-judgment checks in any field. The context label reduces the harm of shipping unvalidated output; the ERROR-corpus evaluation is the route to a real number, and Stage 2 stays out of the pilot except possibly the causal-language rubric once separately validated.
  • Guardrails: the paper's own abstract is never given as context when judging that abstract, and prompts carry explicit caution instructions, since LLM summaries otherwise amplify overclaiming by stripping hedges.
New assembly; individual rubrics anchored in published instruments.

Stage 3 — Robustness (context)

Top model enumerates & screens · cheap models execute
  • The specification space is the union of three sources: the replicas' organic disagreements (from their decision traces); a systematically enumerated decision grid (exclusions, operationalizations, covariates, estimators) built from the methods and data schema; and standard-practice defaults even where the paper is silent (e.g. outlier treatment). The pilot uses a small constrained grid.
  • An adversarial reasonableness screen classifies each specification as defensible or not, logging a rationale for every rejection, so the reader can judge the space rather than just the curve. The screen is itself an evaluation target, not a solved component: 86% of mutually contradictory analyses passed AI review in Miao et al. (78% passed human expert review), and enumeration and screening by related models risks correlated judgment — so the screen is scored against the dev corpora and, where feasible, uses a different model family than the enumerator.
  • Execution goes through existing engines (specr, multiverse, boba — one chosen in the pilot, not all three); PIMA is a candidate for valid inference over the set. Nonconvergent and failed specifications are reported as a category, never silently dropped.
  • The extremeness measure, stated precisely: an extremeness rank of the paper-stated specification (as reconstructed via the estimand contract — the actual published analysis path is unknowable without code) conditional on a declared specification generator, screen, and weighting scheme. Sensitivity across generators, screeners, weights, and failure handling is reported alongside it. The m-value framing (Miao, Pritchard & Zou 2026) and its p-value analogy are adopted only if calibration shows the quantity is stable across those choices.
  • Estimation and interpretation run as separate, differently-prompted stages. A confirmatory prompt flips verdicts from 10% to 90% support while the coefficient distributions stay unchanged, so the bias sits in interpretation, not estimation.
The core novel stage. Kohler et al. name exactly this as future work.

Report

  • One HTML report per paper (claims table with per-quantity match summaries, dispersion statistics, specification curve with conditional extremeness rank, rubric commentary, deterministic-checker appendix), plus a machine-readable JSON sidecar carrying the full artifact chain for batch aggregation.
  • The decision/context separation is stated at the top, along with per-claim outcome states (complete / abstained / human_review_required) and their reasons. The redacted methods document, estimand contracts, and all replica code and traces are attached for audit.

Models & orchestration

  • Multi-model orchestration is the scale strategy. Kohler et al. suggest expensive models may be needed; the bet here is that k cheap replicas from different families plus top-model reasoning stages beat one expensive generalist at batch scale. An escalation path stays open: if calibration shows cheap families underperforming, a top-model replica arm or arbiter is added.
  • Cheap tier (implementation, replicas): glm-5.3-flash ($0.075/$0.25 per MTok; calibration required, since GLM-5 was Kohler's worst configuration at 52% vs 72% cell completion, though 5.3-flash is a newer model) and gpt-5.6-luna ($0.20/$1.20; subscription coverage unconfirmed, test locally).
  • Top tier (extraction arbitration, spec enumeration and screening, matching, diagnosis): GPT-5.6 Sol (subscription-confirmed via Codex CLI) and/or Claude via claude -p.
  • Routing: a standalone Python driver calling headless CLI clients (claude -p, codex exec, opencode run --format json -m provider/model, installed and tested; note the ~17k-token system-prompt overhead per opencode call) plus direct API calls (OpenRouter with price sort and a throughput floor; Z.ai's Anthropic-compatible endpoint). Bring-your-own-subscription is a design requirement.
  • Cost: experiment toward the floor within a design envelope of roughly $20–50/paper. The driver records tokens and cost per run so real numbers are reportable. Seed-rerun requirements for stochastic estimators are part of the cost model, not an afterthought.

Multi100DevelopmentMIT on GitHub; 100 papers, predefined claims, 11k per-analyst results, the only setting where "would humans have diverged too?" is directly answerable. ~47/100 with easily obtainable data; no paper DOIs (title matching needed).

  • Contamination probes: split the dev corpus at each model's training cutoff (the Kohler template). The dev corpora are public, so no dev result is claimed as generalization.
  • Validation sequence: develop on Multi100 ∪ ReplicatorBench, then the discriminant test on ReplicatorBench's non-replicable ground truth, then held-out on I4R×PS, then (for Stage 2) the ERROR corpus.   • The headline comparison to aim for: agent crowd vs analyst crowd on identical papers. Multi100's 34% recovery within ±0.05 d is the human reference band, as orientation, not a performance target — the Multi100–Holtdirk comparison is not methodologically harmonized.

Phasing

  • v0 pilot — the narrow end-to-end test of the novel idea: one user-selected focal estimand per paper; tabular data with a codebook required; OLS/GLM only; Stage 0 with estimand contracts and data readiness, blinded replicas with decision traces, a small constrained specification grid; abstention as a normal outcome. Run on 3–5 Multi100 papers. This doubles as the model-calibration experiment.
  • v1: all abstract claims, wider analysis families, the deterministic-checker appendix, the full enumerated multiverse with sensitivity reporting, Stage 2's three rubrics (context-labeled), batch mode over the dev corpora.
  • Phase 2: the re-execution arm (environment reconstruction is its own engineering problem), the ERROR-corpus Stage-2 evaluation, I4R×PS held-out run, codebook-less input as a measured failure mode.

Risks and limits

  • Deliberate deviations from the evidence base, run as experiments. Three scope choices go against findings in the research synthesis and are flagged as such: unattended operation (the evidence favors binding human gates — mitigated by outcome states and measured abstention/silent-error rates); Stage 2 shipping before a gold-set validation (mitigated by the context label, rubric constraints, and pilot exclusion); and building the multiverse without original code to mine decision points from (the re-derivation premise itself — mitigated by decision traces and the constrained pilot grid). If the measurements go against these bets, the design reverts.
  • Prior-art proximity. Stages 0–1 are substantially Kohler et al.'s design. The contribution claims are confined to the three deltas above and must stay that way in any writeup.
  • Cheap-replica quality is unproven. Kohler's grid puts GLM-class models last. The first milestone is a calibration run before committing the family lineup.
  • The extremeness rank inherits its generator and screen. It is conditional by construction; the sensitivity reporting and rejection log make that legible without removing it. Correlated enumerator/screener judgment is the specific failure mode to watch.
  • Blinding is layered, not guaranteed. The deterministic scan catches exact values only; the typed policy and semantic audit narrow the gap, and the audit quantifies what remains.
  • Contaminated dev corpora. All open ground-truth corpora are public; only I4R×PS approximates held-out.
