## Source extraction: the accuracy problem remains open

The two fresh lanes returned 130 and 108 candidates and inventories for all nine pages. There are 68 pairs, so **57.1% of candidates participate in a pair**. Of those pairs, 62 agree on value, comparator, printed precision, and statistic kind. None agree exactly on every semantic field. Overlapping disputes include 57 outcome labels, 67 contrast labels, and 66 model labels. These figures measure pairing and conditional agreement, not source precision or recall.

The pipeline separates occurrence identity from numerical agreement, preserves comparators and precision, requires source evidence and region inventories, rejects ambiguous matches, and records bounded arbitration. Of 166 retained records, 99 pass source gates and 67 are excluded: 20 duplicate occurrences, 39 unlocated quotations, two non-unique quotations, three numeric/operator/kind or unresolved-adjudication failures, and three parameter-label conflicts with p-value coding. Excluding these records prevents unsupported grading; it does not demonstrate that the remaining records are correct.

[Error strata](../runs/Petersen_Cognition_2017_yJwG_v4_20260913/source_error_strata.json) localise 26 of the 39 unlocated quotations to page 6. Each lane has ten figure candidates but only two pair. Five PDF pages contain unsupported control characters and 14 fonts lack Unicode maps. This supports investigating reading order and figure identity; it does not establish that OCR or normalisation would recover the correct targets. Greek/Latin symbols remain distinct.

The next engineering work should test source-linked semantic inventories, layout-aware source spans, and explicit figure-member identities against independently labelled examples. Inventories should be assigned independently in each extraction lane before reconciliation, so shared labels do not manufacture agreement. The acceptance set must include complete held-out pages and targets missed by both lanes. A higher pairing percentage is insufficient.
